# Anchoring Mechanism behind Contact Prediction in ESM
## 1 intro 

Older methods predicted contacts from the MSA. While MSA provides the conservation scores of the aligned residues, the structural information comes from the residues with lower conservations scores. If two residues close in the 3D space, when one mutates, the other is also likely to change to account for the new mutations properties and maintain the same structure. 

Zhang et al. [[plms_learn_evo_stats.pdf]] show similarities between MSA based covariance analysis using linear methods and a categorical jacobian on esm logits. The categorical jacobian tests all possible mutations, records the changes in logits, and uses that for understanding how esm thinks residues interact with each other. The jacobian also had high correlation with the contact head predictions. 

Additionally, Zhang et al. [[plms_learn_evo_stats.pdf]] showed that contact prediction for distant secondary-structure elements (SSEs) in a partially masked sequence stays near random until a critical number of residues flanking the SSEs are unmasked, at which point accuracy "jumps'' to a significantly higher value. They use this to propose that ESM2 uses local motifs for contact prediction. But they couldn't say which residues within the flanking region are critical, or how the model processes them. Their Equation 1, P(contact[a,b] | seq[a±s], seq[b±s]), treats the entire flanking window as a black box. So our task is contact(SS1, SS2), where SS1 and SS2 are segments of 10 AAs which are about 100 AAs away from each other in the sequence space. The task is to predict contact(SS1, SS2 | flkL, SS1, SS2, flkR), where flkL and flkR are the flanking residues to the outward sides of SS1 and SS2 respectively. Consider the task as a single contact prediction:  $\text{contact}(A,B | X_{1:5}A....BY_{1:5})$ where X and Y are unmasked residues and the residues between A and B, represented by ... are masked. For each AA, we have its conservation score over a given MSA and its SSE tag. 

hypotheses from zhang et al:
(1) learns folding physics (2) look up entire folds for each family (3) look up segment pairings of interacting fragments 
Then they claim that the jump in performance after hitting the flank + similarity between MSA based methods imply (3). 

The categorical jacobian is proposed as a method to extract coevolution signal from pLMs. Average long-range P@L/2 of inv covariance 0.67 vs. cat jacob 0.80 vs contact head 0.87. Their proposed mechanism says the model learns $P(\text{contact}[a, b]|\text{seq}[a : a ± s], \text{seq}[b : b ± s])$, where s depends on the motif the contact is in. Further they propose that the model stores coevolutionary statistics of protein families. This is possible if masked language modeling enables storing of coevolutionary stats. Learns pairwise dependencies conditioned on sequence motifs and relative separation between sequences. When predicting P(A | everything + B), if B and A are in contact, B should have a direct effect on P(A) - as there is a coevolution statistical dependency. but there are other dependencies. If $X_{1:5}A$ is always conserved, A doesn't need B. So X has to vary and be useful for predicting A. Then, to an extent, mutation effect prediction and contact prediction should be highly related.

What should be the search direction?

The direction `W_Q^T @ q_mean` answers: "what residual stream content makes a position _query_ like the average query?" That's the query-side feature — it's the same everywhere (that's why q_mean_norm is 0.93), so it's not what distinguishes the anchor.

The direction that makes the anchor rank #1 is the **key-side** direction: `W_K^T @ q_mean`. This answers: "what residual stream content makes a position produce a key vector that scores highly against all queries?"

The anchor feature is what makes a KEY attractive to queries, not what makes a QUERY look for the anchor.

## 6 Search direction

$q^{LH} = x^{LD} . (W_Q^T)^{DH} + 1^{L1}. (b_Q^T)^{1H}$
$k^{LH} = x^{LD} . (W_K^T)^{DH} + 1^{L1}. (b_K^T)^{1H}$

for each pos $l$, we get query norm,
$n_l^{1} = ||q_l^{H}||_2$

And the corresponding unit query vector,
$\hat{q_l}^{H}= \frac{q_l^{H}}{n_l}$

Stacking across positions gives $\hat{q}^{LH}$. We then compute the mean unit query,
$\bar{q}^H = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{l=1}^{L}{\hat{q_l}^H}$

And normalizing once more
$\tilde{q}^{H}= \frac{\bar{q}^{H}}{||\bar{q}^H||_2}$

This gives an overall query direction for the head. The second normalization removes the magnitude of the mean and keeps only its direction, so the final search direction depends on the shared orientation of the per-position queries rather than on how strongly aligned they happened to be on this sequence.

Finally, map this query-side direction into residual-stream space using the key weights, 
$d^D = (W_K^T)^{DH} . \tilde{q}^H$

This tries to find the residual stream content that makes a position produce a key vector that scores highly against all queries?

Steering against the search direction

Take one candidate key position (j) at the layer-10 input residual $(x_j \in \mathbb{R}^D)$.

For any query ($q_i$), the pre-RoPE logit to that position is
$a_{ij} = q_i^\top k_j$

with
$k_j = x_j W_K^\top + b_K.$

So
$a_{ij} = x_j^\top (W_K^\top q_i) + q_i^\top b_K$

Now define
$d_i = W_K^\top q_i$

Then the logit is linear in (x_j):
$a_{ij} = x_j^\top d_i + \text{const}$

If queries are all aligned, then (d_i) is nearly the same for all (i), and your mean-query construction gives a single selector direction  
$d = W_K^\top \tilde q$

So for the aggregate query, the “anchor evidence” at position (j) is just
$s_j = x_j^\top d$

That means:
- the gradient of the selector score with respect to the residual is exactly (d)
- the steepest local way to reduce “anchorhood” is to move in direction (-d)

$x_j' = x_j - \operatorname{proj}_d(x_j)$
where
$\operatorname{proj}_d(x_j) = \frac{x_j^\top d}{|d|_2^2} d$
After the edit,
${x_j'}^\top d = 0$

### 6.4 the detector direction `d` behaves like a selector axis

What goes in:

- among anchors, amino-acid identity does not explain alpha well
- among anchors, the measured structural features correlate only weakly with alpha
- among anchors, `d` is poorly aligned with the major PCA axes, so it is not a dominant axis of variation within the anchor class
- the most defensible interpretation is: `d` is important for deciding **whether a residue is anchor-like at all**, but it does not explain much of the variation **among residues that already count as anchors**
####  Supporting result A: information-hub behavior is **real but sparse**

This is useful, but I would not let it dominate the report.

What goes in:

- the original information-hub experiment was mixed and noisy, especially on mean influence and on the tiny EVcouplings subset.
- the improved experiment showed the important thing: **top-3 pooled anchors beat matched controls much more clearly on top25 and max-type metrics than on mean metrics**, across target sets. That means anchors strongly help a small subset of targets rather than weakly helping everything.

Why it matters:

- it tells you something about downstream function
- it supports the idea that anchor selection is consequential
- but it is not the cleanest route to the universal feature itself

I would keep this in a later section or as a “functional consequence” section.

#### Supporting result B: the public layer-8 SAE is **not the right lens**

This should stay, but as a negative result / methods lesson.

What goes in:

- the first SAE pass looked dramatic because the error node dominated anchor projections, but after the fixes the interpretation changed
- the key cleanup was: the public layer-8 SAE can represent `d` in span, so the earlier greedy reconstruction failure was an artifact, but anchors are just globally high-error points for that SAE, not specifically hard to reconstruct along `d`
- the safe conclusion is: this public layer-8 SAE does not expose the anchor detector in a clean sparse vocabulary

Why it matters:

- it tells the reader you checked the obvious interpretability lens and it did not work
- it prevents overclaiming around SAE stories

I would include this in methods/negative results or appendix, not as a central figure.
